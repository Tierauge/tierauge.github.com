Glaucoma is excessively high pressure inside the eye. Left untreated, it damages the optic nerve and
retina within a few hours – an acute glaucoma attack is therefore an emergency in which every hour
counts.

## When you should see us

- a suddenly red, cloudy or unusual-looking eye
- pupils of different sizes
- your pet squints one eye, is noticeably quiet or eating less
- glaucoma has already been diagnosed in one eye and the second eye needs to be checked

## What we examine

We measure the intraocular pressure (tonometry) and use the slit lamp and a special lens to look into
the drainage angle (gonioscopy) – the narrow passage through which the fluid inside the eye normally
drains. This lets us tell whether a congenital malformation, inflammation or another cause lies behind
the raised pressure.

## Treatment

The aim is always to lower the pressure, preserve as much vision as possible and reliably relieve pain:
pressure-lowering eye drops, supplemented in acute cases by tablets or infusion, and regular check-ups
to adjust the therapy. For animals at increased risk, we also advise on ways to protect the vision of
the second eye.

If an eye is already blind and remains painful despite treatment, we will discuss with you how to give
your pet lasting freedom from pain – from medically disabling the structure that produces the eye's
fluid (chemical ciliary body ablation), through removal of the eye (enucleation), to the cosmetically
less noticeable intrascleral prosthesis, in which only the inside of the eye is removed and replaced
with a silicone sphere. We will discuss in detail which procedure makes sense in each individual case.
