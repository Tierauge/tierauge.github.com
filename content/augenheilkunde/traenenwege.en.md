A healthy tear film keeps the ocular surface moist and protected. If there is too little tear fluid or
it has the wrong composition (dry eye), or if the tear drainage ducts are narrowed, the ocular surface
suffers permanently.

## When you should see us

- sticky, greyish or yellowish mucus in the eye
- a chronically watering eye without any visible injury
- already visible clouding or pigmentation of the cornea, especially in short-nosed breeds

## What we examine

With the Schirmer tear test we measure tear production to reliably diagnose dry eye. If a narrowed tear
drainage is suspected, we check and flush the tear ducts.

## Treatment

In most cases dry eye is treated long term with eye drops that improve the tear film. Narrowed tear
ducts can be gently widened and flushed with a fine probe; in some cases a small, permanently placed
catheter makes sense. For very severe dry eye that cannot be adequately controlled with medication,
surgery may be considered in individual cases, in which the duct of the parotid salivary gland is
relocated so that saliva helps moisten the ocular surface (parotid duct transposition). This is an
exceptional procedure that we only recommend after detailed consultation.
